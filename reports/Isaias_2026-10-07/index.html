2. Storm status and forecast

Forecast positions and intensities from the NHC advisory layers at the time of the snapshot. Category uses the Saffir-Simpson scale. Local times are Central Daylight Time as labeled by NHC; the UTC column is day/hour.

3. People under each alert

Each block group is assigned the most severe tropical alert covering it, so a person appears once. Where the National Weather Service issued a polygon, the block-group centroid must fall inside it. Zone-based alerts count the whole county. The last row is the national average for comparison.

4. Who is most at risk

Census tracts with the largest at-risk counts in the Hurricane Warning and Storm Surge Warning zones

Rates are per tract. Counts for diabetes, COPD and heart disease are CDC PLACES prevalence × adults in the tract. Poverty and no-vehicle rankings require at least 1,500 residents. Click a tract name to jump to it on the map.

5. Counties

Sorted by alert tier, then by people in that tier. "In tier" is the population inside the county's most severe alert; "any alert" is everyone in the county under at least one tropical alert.
